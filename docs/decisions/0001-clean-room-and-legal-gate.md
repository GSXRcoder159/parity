2. **C2 Rate and read budget:** aggregate sandbox traffic ≤20 req/s across all processes and sandboxes (hard cap); nightly default ≤10 req/s; never load-test (defined in R7). **Reads:** ≤10,000 `GET` requests per Stripe account per rolling 30 days, counted by the shared limiter, until Stripe Support confirms in writing that sandboxes are exempt or grants an increase. The rate-limits page says read requests "must not exceed" the allocation, which makes it an express Documentation prohibition (§1.1, §2.1(iii), Billing §4.2), not a discouragement. **This constrains the harness design:** at ≥2 `GET /v1/events` polls per sequence (`docs/research/2026-09-25-w0-verifications.md` §5), a 500-sequence nightly corpus uses up the 10,000 floor within days. Before nightly runs, either get Support's answer or move event collection to webhook delivery (not a read request).
   - **Stripe Support answer (B4, received 2026-09-27; the reply is summarized here, not quoted).** It restated the policy: an average of 500 reads per transaction over 30 days, with a minimum of 10,000 per month. It did **not** say whether sandboxes are exempt, or whether sandbox test charges count as transactions. **The cap therefore stays at 10,000 GETs per rolling 30 days.** A precise follow-up question is open (B4b): "Do test-mode charges in a sandbox count as transactions for the read allocation?" If they do, our ratio (about 4–5 GETs per charge, W0) is about 1% of the allowance.
   - **Load-testing guidance** (docs.stripe.com/rate-limits, re-read 2026-09-27; already quoted in the README): Stripe discourages load testing in a sandbox and recommends a configurable mock for load tests. Parity never load-tests: ≤20 req/s, about 7.5 requests per sequence, one-seventh of the corpus per night. Its purpose is the mock Stripe recommends, with a measured fidelity number.
   - **Consequence:** the nightly slice must fit the budget. The rolling total is currently about 1,900, and oracle v1 plus a noise pair add about 2,000. A fixed one-seventh slice (about 230 GETs) plus the weekly noise pair (about 300) would reach about 10,000 within 30 days. So nightly sizes tonight's slice to `min(1/7 of the corpus, (10,000 − 1,500 reserve − rolling30) / 7)`, continuing the rotation where it stopped, and the weekly noise pair runs only if it fits the same allowance.

## Analysis per activity

**(a) Clean-room Stripe-compatible twin — GO WITH CONDITIONS.** Read literally, §2.4 forbids creating "derivative works of all or any part of the Stripe Technology or any services provided by Stripe", and §12 puts "the Stripe API" inside Stripe Technology, so an API-compatible twin is within the clause's words. The contract does not define "derivative works" and has no interoperability or clean-room carve-out; the only carve-out is "to the extent ... not permitted under Law". A second clause reaches the twin's other input: our sandbox recordings are "data that User obtains via the Services", i.e. Stripe Data (§12, including "information relating to the Stripe API interactions"), which Stripe owns (§5.1(a)) and which we "will use ... only as expressly permitted by this Agreement" (§4.2). Nothing in the Agreement expressly permits building a separately distributed product from it. Arguments against both readings (arguments, not safe harbors): Parity copies no Stripe code; §1.1 and §2.1 permit use for our "Business Purposes", defined to include activities relevant to our "commercial ... mission"; the spec is MIT and §2.3 lets an open-source license "supersede" the Agreement "regarding open source code" (this covers the spec file, not behavior learned from probes); Stripe ships stripe-mock, recommends it in its Billing testing docs, recommends "a configurable system for mocking out requests to the Stripe API", and tells regression suites to "use a playback testing tool such as the VCR gem" (recording responses for testing is contemplated use, though redistribution of a twin goes further); and where Stripe wanted to bar substitutes built from a product's output it wrote so expressly (Orchestrated Services, Identity, quoted above), with no such clause for Billing. Exposure: suspension or termination of the account we probe from (§10.1(b)); Stripe's own damages for a plain §2.4 or §4.2 breach are capped at Fees paid in the prior 12 months (§8.4; $0 if the claiming account has paid none) unless characterised as willful misconduct after this documented reading (Excluded Claims (a)); but §11.15 names injunctive relief for material breach of Sections 2 and 4, so the realistic worst case is an order or demand to stop distributing the twin, not only a lost oracle. §4 survives termination for as long as we hold recordings (§10.3(c)). Residual risk stays open until counsel review (C7).

**(b) Automated probes against our own sandbox — GO WITH CONDITIONS.** No clause names scraping, automated access or load testing. The Documentation is binding (§1.1, §2.1(iii), Billing §4.2). The rate-limits page discourages sandbox load tests (a discouragement, which we treat as binding) and, separately, says read requests "must not exceed" an allocation with a 10,000-per-month floor. That is an express prohibition, so it is contractually binding through Billing §4.2; whether it applies to sandboxes is unverified, so C2's read budget applies until Stripe answers. Stripe's docs affirmatively invite automated CI use of sandboxes. Probing is not clean under §2.4, though: probes whose purpose is to learn behavior for the twin (W1–W3 recordings, align-loop probes) are the most literal fit for "reverse engineer ... any services provided by Stripe", and the conditions here do not cure that. It is the same open question as (a) and goes to counsel (C7). Pure scoring runs of third-party targets against the sandbox are less exposed. The clauses to stay clear of: §1.2(a)(ii) (circumventing technical limitations: never spread load or reads over extra sandboxes or accounts to beat a limit; only call paths in the public spec), §1.2(a)(iv) and the §10.1(b)(i)(4) suspension trigger (the C2 caps, 429 backoff), §1.2(a)(v) and the "enable or allow any third party" lead-in (C8: no runs for others on our account), and §1.4 (no Preview Services: pin `2026-08-26.dahlia`, send no preview versions).

**(c) Publishing fidelity scores (sandbox noise floor and our twin) — GO WITH CONDITIONS, public release after counsel (C7).** No clause names benchmarking or publishing measurements, but two clauses reach it. First, §4.2: scores are computed from recordings that are Stripe Data ("information relating to the Stripe API interactions"), usable "only as expressly permitted", and publication is not expressly permitted. Aggregate scores, CIs, method, normalization rules, seeds, harness code and divergence descriptors (JSON path + class) are our own analysis *derived from* Stripe Data. That is the strongest position, but it is a counsel question, not a settled one. Second, §1.2(a)(vi): republishing "any part of the Services, Documentation" is barred "except as Law permits", and a §1.2 breach is an Excluded Claim (uncapped). Bulk raw response bodies are the closest fit for both clauses, so C3 applies. Stripe Data also includes "information Stripe uses for security or fraud prevention", so fraud and risk fields in responses (e.g. charge `outcome`) are never published, even as excerpts. Quotes of Stripe docs in the README stay short, attributed and linked. Present the noise floor factually (Marks "disparaging" Don't); the plan's "as documented" framing already fits.

**(d) Publishing stripe-mock / localstripe scores — GO WITH CONDITIONS.** stripe-mock is MIT and localstripe GPL-3.0; neither license restricts benchmarking and neither project has other terms. Conditions (plan §7, §8): score only surfaces each claims; frame stripe-mock's stateful score with its own README quote; state each target's exact version. stripe-mock embeds the legacy `openapi/spec3.json` (`2026-09-30.endive`) while the twin pins `2026-08-26.dahlia`, so the report must say so and not count version-only differences as fidelity failures without a note.

**(e) Scoring Arga / WonderTwin / LocalStack — split.** *Arga:* no published terms were found, so nothing we have read bars private measurement on the free tier with our own data (GO), provided the signup flow is re-checked when the account is created. The only consent text found is a JS-bundle string, and a search snippet (unconfirmed; `docs/research/2026-09-25-w0-verifications.md` §4) says the login portal refers to a ToS. If signup shows or links terms, read them before running anything. Publishing still needs Arga's written agreement (plan §5 cut list) and a re-check for new terms at publish time; never name the "100% fidelity" claim (plan §8). *LocalStack:* **STOP.** Hobby ToS §6 (iii)/(iv) and MSA §2(iv) bar benchmarking and competitive use without express written consent, "Service" covers the locally run Emulator, and the Hobby plan is non-commercial. Parity does not create a LocalStack account or run LocalStack software for scoring without consent from legal@localstack.cloud. *WonderTwin:* the OSS `twin-stripe` is MIT, so running it is licensed. But whether LocalStack's terms now cover it after the 2026-09-14 acquisition is unresolved, and its surface is Connect/settlement rather than Billing v0, so there is nothing fair to score: **STOP** for publication before Nov 2 (consistent with plan §11.5).

**(f) Naming and trademarks — GO WITH CONDITIONS.** The SSA §5.3(a) marks license covers only identifying Stripe as our provider; everything else goes through the Mark Usage Terms (§5.3(c)). Allowed in body text: "Stripe-compatible", "tests against the real Stripe sandbox", "a twin of the Stripe Billing API". Not allowed: "Stripe" inside a product name, repo, npm package, domain, handle, logo or page title; "Stripe Twin" or any "Stripe"+word lockup as a headline or tagline (the "new mark with its own distinct commercial impression" Don't, as well as source-identifying use); possessive, plural or altered forms of the mark in copy (write "the Stripe API", never "Stripe's API"); ™/® next to the mark; the Stripe logo; merchandise; Stripe more prominent than Parity; anything implying endorsement. Any use beyond this goes to trademarks@stripe.com first (the Marks page's stated contact). Current names comply (product "Parity", package `paritybench`, repo `parity`). The README adds: "Parity is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Stripe, Inc. Stripe is a trademark of Stripe, Inc." (this uses no ™/® symbol and no possessive, so it is compliant).

**(g) Vendoring the OpenAPI spec — GO.** MIT; SSA §2.3 makes the open-source license prevail over the SSA "regarding open source code"; the spec comes from GitHub, not "via the Services", so it is not Stripe Data; the README adds no terms; the spec's `info.termsOfService` link is metadata, not a license condition. Vendor `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/stripe/openapi/2bab97685e29abae0c241680c2257153e259e50f/latest/openapi.spec3.json` (4,518,735 bytes, `2026-08-26.dahlia`), never the legacy `/openapi/spec3.json` (`2026-09-30.endive` at the same commit). Pin by commit SHA (tags are opaque build numbers), record its sha256, and keep Stripe's MIT notice in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES`. MIT would also permit `fixtures3.json`, but the clean-room rule excludes fixtures as a response source by design, not by license.

**(h) GPL localstripe as a benchmark target only — GO.** GPLv3 §2 permits running the unmodified program; copyleft attaches to conveying it or a derivative, not to reading its HTTP output. Conditions: install at CI runtime from upstream (PyPI/Docker), never vendor, fork, bundle or redistribute it; record the version tested; no localstripe source is consulted when writing twin behavior. Related: project-vera/vera has no license (all rights reserved), so we cite its methods only; exo is MIT (design reference); `stripe/agent-toolkit` is now `stripe/ai` (MIT) for plan §6.4.

## Clean-room rules (checkable)

- **R1 Allowed inputs to twin behavior:** the vendored spec at the pinned SHA; ParityBench recordings from our own sandboxes (these are Stripe Data under §4.2; allowed on the Business Purpose argument in (a), pending counsel); public docs.stripe.com pages (read, not pasted).
- **R2 Forbidden inputs:** `fixtures3.json` or spec fixture bodies as response values; source code of stripe-mock, localstripe, WonderTwin, vera or any Stripe SDK (SDKs may be used as test clients, never as a code source); anything Stripe gave us under confidentiality (Preview programs, support threads, NDAs); non-public Stripe docs or endpoints.
- **R3 Provenance:** every twin behavior change (commit or align-loop patch) cites a spec JSON pointer or a recording/probe id from the sealed log.
- **R4 CI gate:** `grep -rniE "localstripe|stripe-mock|fixtures3|wondertwin" src/twin` is empty, and `git ls-files | grep -i fixtures3` is empty.
- **R5 Dependencies:** no GPL/AGPL or unlicensed package in `package-lock.json` (e.g. `npx license-checker --production --failOn "GPL;AGPL;UNLICENSED"`).
- **R6 Probe scope:** only our own account's sandboxes; only paths present in the vendored spec; `Stripe-Version: 2026-08-26.dahlia`; no Preview versions or headers.

## Open risks

1. **§2.4 scope.** "Stripe Technology" includes "the Stripe API"; the clean-room design mitigates but does not remove this. It covers the probing that feeds the twin, not only the twin. Worst realistic case: suspension of our oracle account, plus a demand or injunction (§11.15) to stop distributing the twin. Losing the oracle alone is survivable: ParityBench stays runnable by anyone on their own account, and the twin runs without an oracle. An injunction against the twin is not survivable, which is why counsel comes before any public release.
2. **§4.2 Stripe Data.** Recordings are Stripe-owned data (§5.1(a)) usable only "as expressly permitted"; the obligation survives account closure while we hold them (§10.3(c)). Using them to build the twin and publishing anything derived from them both rest on the Business Purpose argument until counsel answers.
3. **§1.2(a)(vi) publication** is uncapped (Excluded Claim). Even divergence excerpts quote response values: keep them short and normalized until counsel says otherwise.
4. **Unclaimed sandbox.** The harness plan runs on a claimable `rkcs_test_` key; which terms govern an anonymous sandbox was not read. Claim it under a real Stripe account by B1's deadline (2026-10-02) and before nightly runs, so the SSA read here is the governing text. The account country selects Regional Terms (e.g. a 30-day notice for termination for convenience in some countries); not read for ours.
5. **Read allocation.** If Stripe says the 10,000-reads/month floor applies to sandboxes and will not raise it, nightly-at-500-sequences with `/v1/events` polling must be redesigned (webhooks, a smaller corpus, or weekly runs). It cannot be waived or worked around with extra sandboxes (§1.2(a)(ii)).
6. **Third-party terms move.** Arga may publish a ToS, and LocalStack may extend its terms to WonderTwin explicitly.
7. **Spec version skew** (endive vs dahlia) can make baseline comparisons look unfair if the report does not state versions.
