# W0 clock measurements (2026-09-27)

**Headline.**
- **Advance to `ready`:** median **14.7 s** for 1-month advances and **14.6 s** for 1-year advances (5 each, one at a time). The max was 20.3 s. A subscription renewal takes the same time whether the jump is a month or a year.
- **Parallel clocks:** 5 and then 10 clocks advanced at once all answered 200, with **no 429 and no cap**: median 14.6 s (max 18.0 s) for 5 clocks and 17.3 s (max 22.1 s) for 10.
- **Exception:** an advance that charges a **declining card** stays `advancing` for **208–393 s** (6 clocks). While a clock advances, the sandbox refuses its delete with a 429.
- **Events:** clock-driven events do appear in `GET /v1/events`, 0–6 s after their `created`, and before the clock is `ready`. Their `created` follows the **wall clock, not the frozen time**. The corpus sweep window (`created[gte]=<start − 60 s>`) therefore covers them, and **the sweep needed no fix**.
- **Templates #27–#30:** validated on seeds 1–2. Every step returned 200 once the waits were set from these numbers: one retrieve after 40 s, and 600 s after #30's declined charge (`docs/plan/impl/templates-validation.md`).
- **H0 clock throughput** (≥20 clock sequences/hour in parallel): passes. At concurrency 8, #27/#29 run in 46–86 s, so 8 workers do about 330–620 of them an hour. #30 takes about 11 min, about 44 an hour.

## 1. Seconds from advance to `ready`

**Method (client side).** The clocks ran one at a time. For each, the script sent `POST …/advance` and then `GET /v1/test_helpers/test_clocks/:id` 1 s after each previous answer (a gap of 1.1–1.4 s between polls, the first poll 1.1 s after the advance) until `status=ready`. The number is the time from the advance's answer to the first `ready` answer, so it overstates the true time by up to one poll gap.

| advance | n | seconds to `ready` | median | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 month | 5 | 14.7, 15.0, 14.7, 20.3, 14.7 | **14.7** | **20.3** |
| 1 year | 5 | 14.6, 20.2, 14.6, 14.4, 16.0 | **14.6** | **20.2** |

**Cross-check (server side).** The first serial run (10 more clocks, polled every 0.5 s with event lists in between) lost its client timings to a script bug. Its events still give the clock's own `test_helpers.test_clock.advancing` → `…ready` timestamps (1 s resolution):

| advance | n | seconds (event `created`) | median | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 month | 5 | 16, 14, 15, 16, 13 | 15 | 16 |
| 1 year | 5 | 14, 14, 13, 13, 15 | 14 | 15 |

In the template runs (8 sequences at concurrency 8, 12 advances), the same event timestamps give 6–24 s. The advances that finalize and pay a renewal draft took 6–8 s; the renewals and cancels took 10–24 s.

**The advance response.** 200, with `status=advancing`, `frozen_time` still the old time, and `status_details.advancing.target_frozen_time` = the target. Once ready: `status_details={}`, and `frozen_time` = the target. `deletes_after` − `created` = 2,592,000 s (30 days).

**Declined-card advances.** Setup: the template #30 shape (the renewal is charged to `pm_card_chargeCustomerFail`). The advance goes from the renewal to the draft's `automatically_finalizes_at` (+3 days); within it, the draft is finalized and its charge fails. Timings were measured three ways:

| run | clocks | how measured | seconds to `ready` |
|---|---|---|---|
| measurement | 2 | polled every 2 s for the first minute, then every 10 s | 215, 328 |
| #30 in the template runs | 2 | clock event timestamps | 208, 334 |
| first #30 attempt | 2 | clocks left advancing, listed every 15 s until ready; bounded windows | 224–239 and 378–393 |

Across the 6 clocks the range is **208–393 s**. The events show `charge.failed` and `invoice.payment_failed` within ~11 s of the clock's `advancing` event; the clock then stays `advancing` for minutes. The cause is unknown.

Advancing only to the draft's `next_payment_attempt` (+1 h) took 2–4 s on 2 clocks, but it left the draft unfinalized (`attempt_count=0`). So it is not a shortcut to the declined state.

## 2. Parallel clocks

**Method.** k clocks were set up (monthly, as above). All k advances were then sent at once through the shared 20 req/s bucket: they went out within 0.2 s (k=5) and 0.5 s (k=10) of each other. Every clock was polled every ~1 s until `ready`.

| k | advance answers | 429s | seconds to `ready` | median | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 200 ×5 | 0 | 14.6, 16.9, 14.5, 18.0, 13.3 | 14.6 | 18.0 |
| 10 | 200 ×10 | 0 | 14.2, 15.8, 17.3, 18.7, 19.9, 15.8, 17.2, 20.9, 22.1, 17.2 | 17.3 | 22.1 |

**No concurrency cap or 429 was observed up to 10 clocks advancing at once.** The median rises by about 3 s from 5 to 10 clocks. The only 429s seen in the whole task were one kind, resource-specific: a DELETE of a clock that is still advancing gets `invalid_request_error`, `Stripe-Rate-Limited-Reason: resource-specific`, "Test clock advancement underway - cannot perform modifications". A cap on the number of clocks per account was not tested (at most 10 existed at once).

## 3. Events of clock-driven transitions

**Method.** On the 10 clocks of the first serial run, the script listed `GET /v1/events?created[gte]=<clock start − 60>` (every page) at these points:
- before the advance;
- every ~2 s while advancing;
- every 2 s after `ready`, until 3 lists in a row added nothing (30 s cap);
- once 4 s after the clock's delete.

It kept the events whose `data.object` is the clock, customer or subscription, or belongs to that customer or subscription. For each event it recorded `created` relative to the clock's start and target, and when it was first listed.

- **They appear: yes.** All 10 clocks gave the same 22 events (by type). The renewal gave `invoice.upcoming`, `invoice.created` and `customer.subscription.updated`, plus `test_helpers.test_clock.advancing` and `…ready`. The delete gave `customer.subscription.deleted`, `invoice.marked_uncollectible`, `invoice.updated` (for the renewal draft), `customer.deleted` and `test_helpers.test_clock.deleted`.
- **How long they take.** Events were listed 0–6 s after their own `created` (at a 2 s list cadence). The renewal's `invoice.created` was stamped 7–10 s after the clock's `advancing` event and listed 8.1–10.9 s after the advance request. The `ready` event was listed 13.6–19.1 s after the advance request. Renewal events are therefore visible **before** the clock is ready. The delete's events were listed within 4.3–4.8 s of the DELETE (the first list after it).
- **`created`: the wall clock.** Every event's `created` is the moment it was processed, seconds after the request, not the clock's frozen time. The renewal events carry a `created` 9–13 s after the clock's start, not the one month or one year the clock jumped. The **objects** an advance creates carry the frozen time: the renewal draft's `created` equals the old `current_period_end` exactly (month and year). Its `next_payment_attempt` is `created` + 3,600 s, and its `automatically_finalizes_at` is `created` + 259,200 s (3 days).

**Sweep verdict (brief item 2).** Clock-driven events are stamped with the wall clock, so no clock event falls outside `created[gte]=<corpus start − 60 s>`, wherever the clock is frozen. Future-dated events could have broken the incremental sweep's `ending_before` cursor, and none exist. **The sweep was not changed.** Checked on the template runs: every recording of #27, #29 and #30 holds the events of its clock's transitions and its own `test_helpers.test_clock.deleted`. The corpus sweep ended in 10.3 s of its 30 s cap.

## Differences from the twin (for the align loop or the assisted pass; nothing changed here)

1. Event `created`: the sandbox uses the wall clock; the twin stamps each advance event with its transition's frozen time.
2. `invoice.upcoming` at renewal: the sandbox sends it; the twin does not.
3. Renewal draft `automatically_finalizes_at`: `created` + 3 days on the sandbox, + 1 h in the twin. `next_payment_attempt` = + 1 h on both.
4. Clock delete with a draft or open invoice: the sandbox sends `invoice.marked_uncollectible` and `invoice.updated`; the twin sends neither.
5. A clock that is advancing refuses its delete with a 429 on the sandbox. The twin is `ready` at once, so this cannot happen there.
6. Declined renewal: the sandbox's next attempt comes 1.77–1.90 days later (Smart Retries; 2 clocks). The twin uses a fixed 2 days.

## Limitations

- The client-side timings carry up to one poll gap (1.1–1.4 s) of error; the event timestamps have 1 s resolution.
- Every measured clock carries one customer and one subscription. Larger clocks may advance more slowly (the docs say so), and the templates never have more than one.
- The declined-card range (208–393 s) comes from only 6 clocks, and its cause is unknown. The 600 s wait is 1.5× the max seen, not a bound.
- Not measured: a cap on the number of clocks per account; a second advance while advancing; objects created on a clock frozen in the past.
