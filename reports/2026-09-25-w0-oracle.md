# W0 oracle measurements (2026-09-25)

**Headline.**
- **Throughput:** at concurrency 8, the sandbox ran the 60-sequence non-clock corpus twice with zero 429s, at **3,950 and 4,436 sequences/hour** on wall time.
- **Noise floor:** the two clean runs, A and D, give **L1 100%, L3 (normalized v1.1) 92.4% [89.4–94.6], so a 7.6% floor**. All 32 of the steps that disagree trace to 6 identified causes, and 5 of them are normalization gaps.
- **Claimable key:** it has an **undocumented request quota**. Concurrency 16, and a second run 3 minutes after that, drew 429 storms. The quota had lifted 18 minutes after the last 429.
- **Read budget:** it caps a 500-sequence corpus at about 9–10 runs per 30 days.
- **Verdict on "nightly": no-go today.** Throughput is not the limit. The read budget and the claimable key are.

- **Clean runs (A, D).** The steps phase ran at 14–15 req/s. Step latency in A was p50 217 ms, p90 969 ms and max 3.9 s. The event sweep is a near-fixed cost: a 3 s wait, then a pass every 3 s until the event count stops changing (30 s cap). It took 18–27 s here and is amortized over a larger corpus. Each clean run observed 627 events (10.45 per sequence) and 28 `charge.succeeded`.
- **The claimable-key quota.** B at c=16 got 429s about 2 s after it started, roughly 577 requests into a 2-minute window that began with A. Every 429 carried this message: *"Claimable sandbox request rate limit exceeded. Claimable sandbox API keys have lower rate limits than regular testmode keys. To get higher rate limits, claim the sandbox to obtain a regular testmode API key."* The responses had no `Stripe-Rate-Limited-Reason` and no `Retry-After` header. [docs.stripe.com/rate-limits](https://docs.stripe.com/rate-limits) documents 25 req/s for sandboxes and nothing about claimable keys.
- **The quota's window.** C used A's settings 3 minutes after B ended and got 429 on 100% of its requests, so this is a quota over a window, not a per-second rate. Single side-effect-free probes (a `POST` to a fabricated missing-customer path, which is not a read) got 429 at 23:43:36 and 404 (cleared) at 00:01:31. **The quota lifted within 18 minutes of the last 429.** D then ran clean. The exact size and window are undocumented. What we observed: about 460 requests in 1 minute is fine, and about 577 in 2 minutes is not.
- **No circuit breaker.** The runner has none, so B and C sent 966 retries into a key that was already limited. See "Harness follow-ups" below.
- **Sustained throughput on this key cannot reach a 500-sequence corpus** (about 3,700 requests) without pacing across quota windows. With a claimed `sk_test_` key, the documented limit is 25 req/s, and A and D suggest about 4,000+ sequences/hour at c=8 under the 20 req/s bucket.

## Read budget (decision 0001 C2)

Every GET is counted, including GETs that got a 429. The ledger is `budget/reads.jsonl`.

- **GETs per sequence: 1.95–2.18.** In D and A respectively, that is 1.48 from template GET steps plus 0.47–0.70 from the event sweep. The sweep pages every event since the corpus started minus 60 s: 627 events, 7 pages per pass, 4–6 passes, so 28–42 GETs, or about 0.045–0.067 GET per event. The CLI's pre-run projection ("GET steps + 20 for the sweep", per the brief) came to 109 against 117–131 actual. It underestimates the sweep above about 300 events. The ledger records actual counts.
- **GETs per charge: 4.2–4.7** (117–131 GETs / 28 `charge.succeeded`). The Stripe documentation gives the allocation as "an average of 500 per transaction". If sandbox test charges counted as transactions (question B4), we would use under 1% of that allowance.
- **Budget per night: 10,000 / 30 ≈ 333 GETs**, which is **about 150–170 sequences per night** at this template mix. Development, validation and baseline runs draw on the same budget.
- **A 500-sequence corpus** costs about 740 step GETs plus 210–320 sweep GETs (about 5,200 events, 53 pages per pass, 4–6 passes). That is **about 950–1,060 GETs per run**, or about 9–10 runs per 30 days with no reads left for anything else. Weekly (4–5 runs, about 3,800–5,300 GETs) leaves roughly half the budget for development.
- **Ledger now: 1,235 GETs in the rolling 30 days.** That is the 800-GET pre-ledger estimate plus A 131, B 123, C 64 and D 117. B's and C's `charges: 0` are artifacts of their failed sweeps, not observations.
- **If Stripe answers "sandboxes are exempt"** (or that test charges count as transactions): the read cap disappears. The binding limits then become the rate (C2: ≤20 req/s, and ≤10 req/s nightly) and the claimable-key quota (gone once claimed). At 10 req/s and about 7.5 requests per sequence, a 500-sequence corpus takes about 6–7 minutes plus the sweep, so nightly or more often is easy.

## Go/no-go on "nightly"

**No-go today, for two independent reasons:**
1. **Read budget:** 10,000 GETs per 30 days allows about 150–170 sequences per night, not a 500-sequence nightly corpus. Until B4 is answered, the schedules that fit are **weekly at 500 sequences** or **nightly at ≤100–150 sequences** (a stratified subset), leaving room for development.
2. **Claimable key:** its quota admits about one 60-sequence corpus per quota window. A 500-sequence run (about 3,700 requests) would have to be paced over hours, and the quota is undocumented and could change.

**The path to go:** claim the sandbox (B1). That lifts the quota and enables the clock stratum. Then get an answer from Stripe on sandbox read allocation (B4), which lifts the read cap. Throughput itself is not the constraint.

## Harness follow-ups (outside Task 5's files)

- `src/bench/run.ts`: add a circuit breaker. After N consecutive steps that end in 429 after all retries, abort the corpus (and still ledger the requests sent). Also record `Stripe-Rate-Limited-Reason`/`Retry-After` when present.
- `src/bench/normalize.ts` v1.2: drop `error.request_log_url`. Treat `updated` as a timestamp key. Add card-network ids, `outcome.risk_score` and refund `destination_details.card.reference` as opaques. Make the list-order tie-break robust to second boundaries.
- The CLI's sweep projection (+20) should scale with expected events, about 1 GET per 100 events per pass.
