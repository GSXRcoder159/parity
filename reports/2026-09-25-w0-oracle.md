# W0 oracle measurements (2026-09-25)

**Headline.** At concurrency 8, the sandbox ran 60 non-clock sequences with zero 429s, a rate of 3,950 sequences/hour on wall time. The claimable sandbox key then hit an **undocumented request quota** after about 577 requests in about 2 minutes. Every later request got a 429, and the quota had not reset 8 minutes later. As a result, **the noise floor was not measured cleanly**, and neither sustained throughput nor clock throughput can be measured until the sandbox is claimed (STATUS B1). The read budget caps a 500-sequence corpus at about 9–11 runs per 30 days. **The verdict on "nightly" is no-go today.** The reasons are below.

The event sweep is a near-fixed cost: a 3 s wait plus passes every 3 s until the event count stops changing, capped at 30 s. It took 26.9 s here and is amortized over a larger corpus.
- **Run B (c=16) tripped a claimable-key limit about 2 s after it started.** Every 429 carried this message: *"Claimable sandbox request rate limit exceeded. Claimable sandbox API keys have lower rate limits than regular testmode keys. To get higher rate limits, claim the sandbox to obtain a regular testmode API key."* The responses had no `Stripe-Rate-Limited-Reason` and no `Retry-After` header. [docs.stripe.com/rate-limits](https://docs.stripe.com/rate-limits) documents 25 req/s for sandboxes and nothing about claimable keys.
- **Run C used A's settings (c=8) 3 minutes after B ended, and got a 429 on 100% of its requests.** This limit is therefore a quota over a window longer than 8 minutes, not a per-second rate. A single side-effect-free probe (a `POST` to a fabricated missing-customer path, which is not a read) still got 429 at 23:43:36, 8 minutes after onset. Further probes (one every 15 minutes, 4 at most) are logged under "Quota reset" below.
- The runner has no circuit breaker. B and C together sent 966 retries into a key that was already limited. See "Harness follow-ups" below.

## Read budget (decision 0001 C2)

This section uses run A only, the clean run. Every GET counts, including the GETs that got a 429, and the ledger `budget/reads.jsonl` records them all.

- **GETs per sequence: 2.18** (131 / 60). That is 1.48 from template GET steps plus 0.70 from the event sweep. The sweep paged 627 events (10.45 per sequence) in 7 pages per pass over 6 passes: 42 GETs, or about 0.067 GET per event. The CLI's pre-run projection ("GET steps + 20 for the sweep", per the brief) came to 109 against 131 actual. It underestimates the sweep for corpora above about 300 events. The ledger records actual counts.
- **GETs per charge: 4.7** (131 GETs / 28 `charge.succeeded`). Stripe's allocation is "an average of 500 per transaction". If sandbox test charges counted as transactions (question B4), we would use under 1% of that allowance.
- **Budget per night: 10,000 / 30 ≈ 333 GETs**, which is **about 150 sequences per night** at 2.18 GETs per sequence, assuming the #1–#15 mix is representative. Development, validation and baselines draw on the same budget.
- **A 500-sequence corpus** costs about 740 step GETs plus 105–320 sweep GETs (about 5,200 events, 53 pages per pass, 2–6 passes). That is **about 850–1,060 GETs per run**, or about 9–11 runs per 30 days with no reads left for anything else. Weekly (4–5 runs, about 4,000–4,800 GETs) leaves about half the budget for development.
- **Ledger now:** 1,118 GETs in the rolling 30 days. That is the 800-GET pre-ledger estimate plus A 131, B 123 and C 64. B's and C's `charges: 0` are artifacts of their failed sweeps, not observations.
- **If Stripe answers "sandboxes are exempt"** (or that test charges count as transactions): the read cap disappears, and the binding limits become the rate (C2: ≤20 req/s, and ≤10 req/s nightly) and the claimable-key quota (gone once claimed). At 10 req/s and 7 step requests per sequence, a 500-sequence corpus takes about 6 minutes plus the sweep, so nightly or more often is easy.

## Go/no-go on "nightly"

**No-go today, for two independent reasons:**
1. **Read budget:** 10,000 GETs per 30 days allows about 150 sequences per night, not a 500-sequence nightly corpus. Until B4 is answered, the fitting schedules are **weekly at 500 sequences** or **nightly at ≤100–150 sequences** (a stratified subset), with the rest of the budget left for development.
2. **Claimable key:** it cannot sustain even a second 60-sequence corpus within 10 minutes, so no scheduled run is reliable on it.

**The path to go:** claim the sandbox (B1). That lifts the quota and enables the clock stratum. Then get Stripe's answer on sandbox read allocation (B4), which lifts the read cap. Throughput per se is not the constraint: at c=8 it is about 8× the H0 threshold.

## Harness follow-ups (outside Task 5's files)

- `src/bench/run.ts`: add a circuit breaker. After N consecutive steps that end in 429 after all retries, abort the corpus (and still ledger the requests sent), so a limited key does not absorb hundreds of retries. Also record the `Stripe-Rate-Limited-Reason` and `Retry-After` response headers when present.
- `src/bench/normalize.ts` v1.2: treat `updated` as a timestamp key, and make the list-order tie-break independent of second boundaries. Sort pages whose elements all share one sequence's creation burst by normalized content. Alternatively, drop `created` from the sort key when the elements' `created` values differ by ≤1 s.
- The CLI's sweep projection (+20) should scale with expected events, about 1 GET per 100 events per pass.
