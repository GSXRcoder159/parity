Questions

What does “private alignment” mean?

The public twin is aligned to our own sandbox and a public corpus of call sequences. Private alignment aligns it to the call shapes your code actually makes, on the twin's current surface. Every run for you uses your own Stripe account and your own sandbox key, never ours. Aligning the twin to your account config is not offered yet: it is cut from the current plan and comes back when a letter of intent asks for it.

Why is the open score free?

A fidelity score only means something if anyone can check it. The harness, the method and the numbers are public, so you can rerun the score on your own account instead of trusting ours. What costs money is work on your own calls: aligning the twin to your call shapes, which the public corpus does not cover.

How does the rolling oracle work?

The oracle is the latest sandbox recording of each sequence in the corpus. Until Stripe confirms whether its read allocation applies to sandboxes, we cap sandbox reads at 10,000 per account per rolling 30 days. Re-recording the whole corpus every night would exceed that, so each night we re-record a slice (sequence index mod 7 = day of week), and the whole corpus refreshes weekly. The twin is then scored on the whole corpus against the latest recording of each sequence. So the twin is scored nightly against an oracle that is at most 7 days old; drift on any sequence is detected within 7 days.

Today the oracle is v0: 88 sequences, not the planned 520 (day-0 report).
