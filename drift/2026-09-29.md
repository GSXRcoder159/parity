## Triage (2026-09-29)

**Not API drift.** Both causes are in how the two recordings were made, not in what the API returns.

- **The six `description` paths** (subscription-update-price, invoice-preview-proration, subscription-update-quantity): a proration invoice line's description names the calendar day of the request in Stripe's `D Mon YYYY` form (`Unused time on ... after <date>`, `Remaining time on ... after <date>`). The previous recordings of these sequences were made on an earlier day (2026-09-28), so the text differs while the lines' `period` timestamps agree. The one noise pair that covers the proration templates (2026-09-28) recorded both sides minutes apart on the same day, so it could not show this, and normalization up to v1.4 left the text as is.
- **`$.latest_invoice.account_name`** (expand-paths): an account setting. The previous recording was made on 2026-09-26, while the sandbox was unclaimed and invoices carried no account name (null). Since the claim on 2026-09-28, invoices carry the account's display name.

**Fixed in normalization v1.5** (commit 47b465b). Rule `readable-dates` replaces a `D Mon YYYY` date inside any string with `<date>`; the dates are still compared in the timestamp fields. `account_name` joins rule `per-account-opaques`, null included. Each rule states what it can hide (`src/bench/normalize.ts`). Replaying this night's drift on its 72 train sequences, against the oracle as it stood before the night, gives 5 rows under v1.4 and none under v1.5. The two subscription-update-quantity rows are on a held-out template, whose recordings were not opened for this triage; they are the same `description` paths.

**What the instrument got right.** It flagged a real change in account state: claiming the sandbox changed what invoices return, and drift caught it the first time a claimed recording met an unclaimed one. It also found a normalization gap that the same-day noise pairs could not: text that depends on the day of the recording.
