## Triage (2026-10-01)

**Not API drift.** 2026-10-01 is the first night in a new month, and every path holds a value that depends on the month the sequence was recorded in. The previous recordings of these sequences were made in September (2026-09-26, 2026-09-28 and 2026-09-29).

- **`exp_month` (39 paths):** a payment method's card, on attach, list, detach, in a declined subscription's error, and in a charge's `payment_method_details`. Stripe's test cards expire in the month of the request, one year on. Every train oracle recording agrees, September's and tonight's. `exp_year` will move the same way on 1 January.
- **Timestamps one calendar month out (most of the other paths):** `current_period_end` and `current_period_start`, invoice and line `period` fields, `cancel_at`, `ended_at`, a renewal or preview invoice's `created`, a test clock's `frozen_time` and `target_frozen_time`. A monthly period is 30 days from a September start and 31 days from an October one. Normalization up to v1.5 writes a timestamp as hours from the recording's first `created`, so the same period end was `+720h` in September and `+744h` tonight.
- **Timestamps one month plus a fixed time (the 7 paths below):** the same cause, offset by an hour or by days after the period end.

**Fixed in normalization v1.6** (commit e639714). Rule `calendar-months` writes a timestamp within an hour of a whole number of calendar months from the anchor as `<t:+1mo>` (`<t:+12mo>` for a year), and leaves every other offset in hours. `deletes_after`, `due_date` and `trial_end` stay in hours, because Stripe counts them in days. Rule `card-expiry` replaces a payment method's or a charge's card `exp_month` and `exp_year` with `<opaque>` when they are a valid month and year, so the twin's placeholder 0 is still compared. Each rule states what it can hide (`src/bench/normalize.ts`).

Replaying this night's drift on its 71 train sequences, against the oracle as it stood before the night, gives 85 rows (168 divergences in 46 sequences) under v1.5 and 7 rows (15 divergences in 3 sequences) under v1.6. The same replay of 2026-09-29 gives no rows under either version. The other 21 rows are on held-out templates, whose recordings were not opened for this triage. Their paths are the same kinds as the train rows: `exp_month`, period fields, `cancel_at`, a preview's `created`, and one preview `next_payment_attempt`, which is of the kind that remains.

On the 2026-10-01 oracle, score-twin gives the same L1–L4, train and held-out, under both versions, and the same divergence counts. The calendar-month rule changes nothing there, because the twin replays each sequence at the oracle's own clock. The twin's expiry is 0, which stays a divergence. The next nightly scores under v1.6 and marks the change on its curve point.

**What remains (7 rows, all in v1.6's hour buckets by design):**

- clock-declined-renewal, the renewal invoice's `automatically_finalizes_at`, `effective_at` and `status_transitions.finalized_at`, and the clock's `frozen_time` and `status_details.advancing.target_frozen_time` after the second advance: the sandbox finalizes a renewal invoice 3 days after the period end, so these are one month plus 72 h (`+792h` from a September start, `+816h` from October).
- clock-declined-renewal `$.data[0].next_payment_attempt`, and invoice-preview-proration's preview `next_payment_attempt`: one hour after the period end before any attempt (`+721h` and `+745h`). After the declined attempt, rule `retry-day` rounds the retry to days, and October's extra day shows as `+35d` against `+36d`.

These need a different rule shape, such as an offset from the last whole month before the value (`<t:+1mo+72h>`). That would widen what the month rule hides, so it is not part of v1.6. Until then, these 7 rows will come back whenever a sequence is re-recorded in a month of a different length.

**What the replay caught.** Applied to every timestamp key, the month rule made new drift: a test clock's `deletes_after` is a fixed 30 days after its creation, which from a September start is also exactly one calendar month, so it read `<t:+1mo>` in September and `<t:+720h>` in October (9 rows on the first replay). That is why the day-counted keys keep their hours.
